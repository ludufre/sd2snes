> **What is this?** A version of the [original sd2snes firmware](https://github.com/mrehkopf/sd2snes) by [@mrehkopf](https://github.com/mrehkopf) that keeps the firmware base and adds user-facing extras. Use this repo only for issues about **those additions** — for core firmware issues, use the original project.

## 👉 Full guides, downloads, tools and FAQ: **[sd2snes.ludufre.com](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com)**

Everything below is a quick overview. **The site has the step-by-step guides, screenshots and the web tools** — head there for anything beyond the basics.

## Get it running

1. Download the **full** `.zip` from [**Releases**](https://github.com/ludufre/sd2snes/releases).
2. Copy its contents to the **root of your SD card** (replace when asked).
3. **Fill your library:** with the SD card still in your computer, open the **[Web Manager](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/manager/)** in your browser and connect the card — it scans your ROMs and downloads **box art, game-info cards (with animated video clips) and cheats** straight onto the card. Runs in the browser, nothing to install.
4. Put the card back and power on. The first boot asks you to set the clock — set it then, or later from Settings → Time; if you would rather not be asked at all, switch **Ask for clock on boot** off in Browser Options. Change the language anytime in the **Language** menu.

> [!NOTE]
> No games/ROMs are included — use your own legally obtained files.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Updating from 2.15 or older?** 2.15 moved saves, cheats, savestates, covers and game info into **two-letter folders** (`/sd2snes/saves/SU/Super Mario World.srm`), which is what makes a game open instantly on a card holding thousands of files, and **2.16 gives every console a folder of its own inside those** (`sgb`, `nes`, `sms`, `a26`, `sft`) — a sidecar is named from the ROM's stem, so before this a `Tetris (USA).nes` and a `Tetris (USA).sfc` shared one cover, one game-info card and one set of cheats. Nothing is deleted, but until the card is reorganised those files look like they are gone. The **[Web Manager](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/manager/)** reads the firmware version off the card and organises it accordingly.

| <img src="gfx/diffs/sms-nes.gif" width="320" alt="Master System and NES cores (experimental)"> | **Master System, NES and Atari 2600 (experimental, Mk.III only)**<br>The FPGA learned to become three more consoles: drop a `.sms`, `.nes` or `.a26` on the card and it shows up in the list like any other game. NES mappers 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 (MMC3 and, with an NES 2.0 header, MMC6), 7, 11, 18, 19 (Namco 163), 21, 22, 23, 25 (Konami VRC2/VRC4), 24, 26 (VRC6), 28, 32, 33, 34, 48, 65, 66, 69 (FME-7), 70, 71, 72, 75 (VRC1), 78, 79, 80, 82, 86, 87, 88, 89, 93, 94, 95, 97, 113, 140, 152, 154, 180, 184, 185, 206 (Namco 108), 210 and 232 are supported. It plays NTSC cartridges of 2K and 4K plus the F8, F6 and F4 bankswitching schemes, including their Superchip variants (F8SC/F6SC/F4SC, with the extra 128 bytes of RAM); E0, 3F, FE, FA, DPC and Supercharger carts, PAL games and the paddle/keypad/driving controllers are **not** supported yet. Holding L + R together leaves the switches inert, so the in-game reset combos keep working. Sound is the TIA's two channels with all 16 waveform modes. The picture is drawn in two bits per pixel with the colours changed line by line, and a menu option picks its width: **160 (1:1)**, the console's own resolution with black bars at the sides, or **256**, filling the screen.<br><br>See the [compatibility list](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/gamesdb/compatibility). |

| <img src="gfx/diffs/trainer.gif" width="320" alt="Search the game's memory from the in-game menu"> | **Find your own cheats: the RAM trainer**<br>Nobody published a code for the thing you want to change? Find it yourself. The **TRAINER** tab searches the console's memory while the game sits frozen. Type the number you can see on screen — lives, coins, HP — and it lists every address holding it. Play a bit, reopen the menu, filter again, and the list shrinks until only the right address is left. When the number is not on screen, start an **Unknown** search instead and filter by *changed*, *unchanged*, *increased*, *decreased*, *greater* or *less* — the same way you would hunt an invisible value. Works on 8-bit and 16-bit values.<br><br>Pick an address to see what it holds right now, write a new value once, or **freeze** it so the game cannot change it back (up to four at a time). Addresses you set or froze stay in the results — marked FROZEN while they are held — even when a later filter would drop them, and even across a new search, until you pick **Remove from list**. Freezing does not touch your cheat list: it rides the same engine as the cheats, so it obeys the master switch, and it is gone when you load another game. When you want to keep a find, **Save cheat** turns it into a real cheat at the top of the CHEATS tab and writes it to the game's YAML. Loading a savestate keeps the search going, and the relative filters then compare against the values you just loaded, so the usual "load, lose a life, filter, load again" hunt works. Available wherever the in-game menu is — Mk.II and Mk.III alike. Searches cover the SNES work RAM ($7E0000-$7FFFFF); the extra RAM inside special chips is not scanned. |

Grab ready-made themes in the [gallery](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/gallery) or build your own in the [web editor](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/theme). Since 2.16 those two are yours to set as well, independently of the theme: **Text outline** and **Anti-aliasing** in Browser Options change the menu font on the spot, without reloading the menu. | <img src="gfx/diffs/cheats.gif" width="320" alt="Cheat manager in the menu"> | **Cheat manager in the menu**<br>The sd2snes already applies cheats per game — what sd2snes+ adds is the in-menu manager: from the context menu (Y) you turn each code on or off on the console, without editing the YAML on a PC. The files live under /sd2snes/cheats, grouped into two-letter folders (Super Mario World.yml → /sd2snes/cheats/SU/); grab ready-made cheats at gamehacking.org, exporting as FXPak Pro 1.7 (.yml), or let the Web Manager download and file them for you.<br><br>Since 2.16.4 you can also **type cheats in on the console**: Select adds a new one and Y edits the one under the cursor — both in this list and in the in-game menu's CHEATS tab. An on-screen keyboard shows only the characters a code can use (Game Genie `XXXX-YYYY` or Pro Action Replay `AAAAAADD`, the two formats the firmware already accepts — a legend under the keys reminds you of both), a second keyboard types the optional name, and a cheat can hold several codes. The editor also deletes the cheat, after a confirmation. Every add, edit or delete is written to the YAML at once — a new cheat goes to the top of the file and starts enabled — so a code you found with the trainer, or read off a website, is saved without ever pulling the card. |

| <img src="gfx/diffs/patches.gif" width="320" alt="IPS/BPS patch at boot"> | **IPS/BPS patch at boot**<br>The heart of ROM hacking: apply fan translations, hacks, and fixes without altering the original ROM. Drop the .ips/.bps patch into the ROM’s folder, named after the game plus a suffix of your choice (`Zelda - PT-BR.ips` for `Zelda.sfc`). At launch, the firmware lists every patch it finds. The list scrolls, with D-pad page jumps and L/R to jump to the beginning or end, and the selected patch is applied in memory, leaving the original ROM untouched. In the patch list, each patch can be configured for a headered or headerless ROM, or left on Auto to let the system detect the ROM format automatically. The choice is remembered per patch. Press Y on a patch to open a context menu containing this setting and Create Patched ROM, which writes a ready-patched copy next to the original (It may take a few minutes if guides and manuals are included). |

| <img src="gfx/diffs/delete.gif" width="320" alt="Delete a file and its savegame"> | **Delete a file and its savegame**<br>From the browser's context menu (Y), delete the selected file or just the ROM's save (.srm) — right on the console, without pulling the card out or turning on a PC.<br><br>Since 2.16.2 deleting a ROM also clears what belonged to it: the box art beside it, the info card, screenshot, clip and guides under `/sd2snes/info`, its cheat file and its patch settings. Your saves are never part of that: battery saves, memory packs and savestates stay put, and *Delete save* remains its own action. Deleting a theme, an .spc or a .pcm never touches a game's files, even when the names match. With **Show sd2snes folder** on (Browser Options), the card's own files are listed as well — saves, savestates, cheat files, box art and info cards — and Y deletes them one at a time; the firmware, the menu and the FPGA cores show up too, but with no Delete entry. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/reset.gif" width="320" alt="Reset brings you back where you were"> | **Reset brings you back where you were**<br>A short reset can drop you back in the menu already inside the folder of your last game — or right on the ROM you were playing. Pick it in In-game Settings → Reset to menu: Off, On, Folder or ROM — or **Duration**, added in 2.16, which is the one that keeps the console's own reset button working the way you expect: a **short** press restarts the game you are playing, a **long** one (hold it, or press twice quickly) brings you back to the menu, in the game's folder with the ROM already selected. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/memtest.gif" width="320" alt="Memory test in the menu"> | **Memory test, on the console**<br>Is the cartridge broken, or is it the firmware? Main menu → **Memory test** answers that without a serial cable and without flashing the separate diagnostic image. **A** runs the wiring test (~10 s): it walks the data and address lines of the PSRAM and of the 4 Mbit SRAM looking for open, stuck or shorted lines, and names the chip by its silkscreen — *U501: check data line D3*. **X** runs the full test (~30 s), which adds a write-and-verify pass over every cell and reports the first bad address if it finds one. The screen goes dark and the console resets while it runs — that is expected, the FPGA is loaded with the test core meanwhile — and the menu comes back with the result. Needs `fpga_test.bit`/`.bi3` on the card (it ships with the release); without it the test simply declines, without touching the running console. |
