> **What is this?** A version of the [original sd2snes firmware](https://github.com/mrehkopf/sd2snes) by [@mrehkopf](https://github.com/mrehkopf) that keeps the firmware base and adds user-facing extras. Use this repo only for issues about **those additions** — for core firmware issues, use the original project.

## 👉 Full guides, downloads, tools and FAQ: **[sd2snes.ludufre.com](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com)**

Everything below is a quick overview. **The site has the step-by-step guides, screenshots and the web tools** — head there for anything beyond the basics.

## Get it running

1. Download the **full** `.zip` from [**Releases**](https://github.com/ludufre/sd2snes/releases).
2. Copy its contents to the **root of your SD card** (replace when asked).
3. **Fill your library:** with the SD card still in your computer, open the **[Web Manager](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/manager/)** in your browser and connect the card — it scans your ROMs and downloads **box art, game-info cards (with animated video clips) and cheats** straight onto the card. Runs in the browser, nothing to install.
4. Put the card back and power on. Change the language anytime in the **Language** menu.

> [!NOTE]
> No games/ROMs are included — use your own legally obtained files.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Updating from 2.15 or older?** 2.15 moved saves, cheats, savestates, covers and game info into **two-letter folders** (`/sd2snes/saves/SU/Super Mario World.srm`), which is what makes a game open instantly on a card holding thousands of files, and **2.16 gives every console a folder of its own inside those** (`sgb`, `nes`, `sms`, `a26`, `sft`) — a sidecar is named from the ROM's stem, so before this a `Tetris (USA).nes` and a `Tetris (USA).sfc` shared one cover, one game-info card and one set of cheats. Nothing is deleted, but until the card is reorganised those files look like they are gone. The **[Web Manager](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/manager/)** reads the firmware version off the card and organises it accordingly.

| <img src="gfx/diffs/igmenu.gif" width="320" alt="In-game menu with tabs"> | **In-game menu with tabs**<br>Pause the running game with L + R + Y + ← and get a full menu drawn in your theme, with four tabs: **CHEATS** (toggle codes live), **SAVESTATES** (pick the slot, occupied ones are marked), **SAVES** (battery save status and the SRAM slot selector) and **GUIDES** (the game's manuals on screen). B steps back, START closes, and the game resumes exactly where it left off — it even remembers the tab, the cursor and the page you were reading. Available on special-chip games too — every chip except the Super Game Boy (on SPC7110 the menu works but savestates stay off). |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/savestates.gif" width="320" alt="Savestates on special-chip games"> | **Savestates on special-chip games**<br>Savestates never worked on games with a special chip — now they do: **DSP1/1b/2/3/4** (Super Mario Kart, Pilotwings), **Super FX** (Star Fox, Yoshi's Island), **OBC1** (Metal Combat), **S-DD1** (Star Ocean) and **CX4** (Mega Man X2/X3) on both Mk.II and Mk.III, plus **SA-1** (Super Mario RPG) on the FXPak Pro (Mk.III) only. Save with Start + R, load with Start + L, pick the slot with Select + D-pad or in the SAVESTATES tab. A handful of games need a different combo because Start pauses them — those are listed in `/sd2snes/savestate_inputs.yml` on the card (Donkey Kong Country 1/2/3, Yoshi's Island, Mega Man X/X2/X3 and Star Fox use X + R / X + L; Super Valis IV uses A + R / A + L). It is a plain checksum-to-combo list, so you can add your own games to it. Built on top of the [save states by FURiOUS](https://github.com/mrehkopf/sd2snes/blob/master/README.Savestates.FURiOUS.md). |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/manual.gif" width="320" alt="Read the game manual on your TV"> | **Read the game manual on your TV**<br>Convert a PDF manual, walkthrough, map or insert to `.man` (see `utils/gen_man.py`), drop it in `/sd2snes/info/<BB>/` next to the game's other info files, and read it on the TV with the game frozen — from the GUIDES tab, or straight from the game info card by pressing X before you start. L/R turns the page, A zooms 2x and the D-pad pans across the enlarged page; it remembers where you stopped. Up to 8 documents per game, each with its own title and type. The Web Manager downloads them for you. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/sms-nes.gif" width="320" alt="Master System and NES cores (experimental)"> | **Master System, NES and Atari 2600 (experimental, Mk.III only)**<br>The FPGA learned to become three more consoles: drop a `.sms`, `.nes` or `.a26` on the card and it shows up in the list like any other game. **This is a preview, not a finished feature:** NES games with a battery run but **do not save**, and the Master System does not write to cartridge RAM; compatibility is low and many games freeze or glitch. NES mappers 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 (MMC3), 7 and 28 are supported.<br><br>The **Atari 2600** is the newest and the roughest of the three. It plays NTSC cartridges of 2K and 4K plus the F8, F6 and F4 bankswitching schemes, including their Superchip variants (F8SC/F6SC/F4SC, with the extra 128 bytes of RAM); E0, 3F, FE, FA, DPC and Supercharger carts, PAL games and the paddle/keypad/driving controllers are **not** supported yet. Both joysticks are mapped to the SNES pads — D-pad to move, B/A/Y to fire — and the console switches to the rest: hold **Start** for the console's RESET switch, hold **Select** for SELECT, **X** flips Color/B&W and **L**/**R** flip the left/right difficulty switches. Holding L + R together leaves the switches inert, so the in-game reset combos keep working. Sound is the TIA's two channels with all 16 waveform modes. The picture is drawn in two bits per pixel with the colours changed line by line, and a menu option picks its width: **160 (1:1)**, the console's own resolution with black bars at the sides, or **256**, filling the screen.<br><br>See the [compatibility list](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/gamesdb/compatibility). |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/spc7110.gif" width="320" alt="SPC7110 games running"> | **Far East of Eden Zero and the other SPC7110 games**<br>The SPC7110 is the Hudson coprocessor found in a handful of Japanese carts — *Far East of Eden Zero* (Tengai Makyou Zero), *Momotarou Dentetsu Happy* and *Super Power League 4*. It decompresses graphics in hardware, multiplies and divides for the game, walks a second data ROM through a pointer engine and, on *Far East of Eden Zero*, carries a battery-backed real-time clock. The English fan translation of *Far East of Eden Zero* also works: its 7 MB image adds a third chip — an expansion ROM the board maps at banks $40-4f — which the core maps as well (thanks to [@terminator2k2](https://github.com/terminator2k2) for the original fix). The in-game menu and the cheat overlay work here like on the other chips; savestates are the one thing still off on this chip — the decompressor's internal state cannot be captured yet. The core is written for this fork, with the chip's behaviour taken from the [ares](https://github.com/ares-emulator/ares) C++ implementation. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/fx3.gif" width="320" alt="DOOM (2025) running via Super FX 3"> | **Super FX 3: DOOM (2025) and the Star Fox FX3 patches**<br>**Up front: this does not run at the real cartridge's speed — the sd2snes FPGA cannot match the 2025 chip.** *Star Fox* plays at full speed, but *DOOM* sits at ~11 fps against the cartridge's 20 fps cap, which lands on the feel of the 1995 SNES *DOOM*. The in-game menu, cheats and savestates stay off on FX3 carts. The implementation is original to this fork, written from the publicly released Super FX 3 specification by Randal Linden plus black-box behavioural testing and measurements on real hardware — no code from any other implementation was used. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/sufami.gif" width="320" alt="Sufami Turbo minicarts running"> | **Sufami Turbo minicarts (Mk.II and Mk.III)**<br>The Sufami Turbo is the Bandai passthrough cartridge with **two** minicart slots. Drop the `.st` dumps on the card, add the base cartridge BIOS as `/sd2snes/stbios.bin`, and sd2snes+ boots them — picking the **Slot B** companion cart in a dialog right before the game starts, and remembering the pair for next time. Both minicarts keep their own battery save. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/comp.gif" width="320" alt="Nintendo's tournament carts running"> | **Nintendo's tournament carts: Campus Challenge '92 and PowerFest '94**<br>The cartridges Nintendo built for its official competitions — three timed games and a score screen, driven by a hardware round timer — boot as plain `.sfc` dumps: **Campus Challenge '92** (Super Mario World, F-Zero, Pilotwings) and **PowerFest '94** (Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels, Super Mario Kart, Ken Griffey Jr.). All five circulating dumps are recognized — originals, repros and the score hacks. The round length is the DIP switch bank of the real boards: set it under Chip options → **Competition Cart timer (min)**, 3–18 minutes (6 was the event setting). Needs `dsp1b.bin` on the card (both carts carry a DSP-1). Thanks to [@terminator2k2](https://github.com/terminator2k2), who brought these carts to sd2snes first. |

Drop .thm files in any folder on the card and choose one in the browser. Grab ready-made themes in the [gallery](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/gallery) or build your own in the [web editor](https://sd2snes.ludufre.com/theme). Since 2.15 the **sd2snes+** theme is the factory default, with *Restore classic theme* in Browser Options to bring the old look back; themes can also switch off the text outline and the anti-aliasing, and toggle the selection-bar shadow. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/cheats.gif" width="320" alt="Cheat manager in the menu"> | **Cheat manager in the menu**<br>The sd2snes already applies cheats per game — what sd2snes+ adds is the in-menu manager: from the context menu (Y) you turn each code on or off on the console, without editing the YAML on a PC. The files live under /sd2snes/cheats, grouped into two-letter folders (Super Mario World.yml → /sd2snes/cheats/SU/); grab ready-made cheats at gamehacking.org, exporting as FXPak Pro 1.7 (.yml), or let the Web Manager download and file them for you. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/memorypack.gif" width="320" alt="BS-X carts with a Memory Pack slot"> | **BS-X carts with a Memory Pack slot**<br>Added support for the Satellaview 8M Memory Pack slot for the Japanese games that use it — drop the pack dump at `/sd2snes/saves/<BB>/<rom>.mpk` (&lt;BB&gt; = the first two characters of the ROM name, e.g. /sd2snes/saves/BS/) and the game reads the Memory Pack contents (saved sound novels, downloaded races…). On top of that, Derby Stallion 96 and Sound Novel Tsukuru now work. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/patches.gif" width="320" alt="IPS/BPS patch at boot"> | **IPS/BPS patch at boot**<br>The heart of ROM hacking: apply fan translations, hacks, and fixes without altering the original ROM. Drop the .ips/.bps patch into the ROM’s folder, named after the game plus a suffix of your choice (`Zelda - PT-BR.ips` for `Zelda.sfc`). At launch, the firmware lists every patch it finds. The list scrolls, with D-pad page jumps and L/R to jump to the beginning or end, and the selected patch is applied in memory, leaving the original ROM untouched. In the patch list, each patch can be configured for a headered or headerless ROM, or left on Auto to let the system detect the ROM format automatically. The choice is remembered per patch. Press Y on a patch to open a context menu containing this setting and Create Patched ROM, which writes a ready-patched copy next to the original (It may take a few minutes if guides and manuals are included). |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/descriptions.gif" width="320" alt="A description for every menu option"> | **A description for every menu option**<br>From firmware 2.11 on, every settings option shows a line explaining what it does, right on screen — in Portuguese, English, Spanish, German, French and Italian. It's easy to understand each setting without a manual. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/chips.gif" width="320" alt="Warns when a chip's BIOS is missing"> | **Warns when a chip's BIOS is missing**<br>Some games use special chips (DSP in Super Mario Kart, Super FX in Star Fox, SA-1, S-DD1, OBC1, CX4, Super Game Boy, BS-X…) that need support files on the card. From firmware 2.11 on, if one is missing, instead of freezing or booting a broken game, the menu shows which file is missing and returns to the menu without resetting the console. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/delete.gif" width="320" alt="Delete a file and its savegame"> | **Delete a file and its savegame**<br>From the browser's context menu (Y), delete the selected file or just the ROM's save (.srm) — right on the console, without pulling the card out or turning on a PC. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/reset.gif" width="320" alt="Reset brings you back where you were"> | **Reset brings you back where you were**<br>A short reset can drop you back in the menu already inside the folder of your last game — or right on the ROM you were playing. Pick it in In-game Settings → Reset to menu: Off, On, Folder or ROM. |
| <img src="gfx/diffs/memtest.gif" width="320" alt="Memory test in the menu"> | **Memory test, on the console**<br>Is the cartridge broken, or is it the firmware? Main menu → **Memory test** answers that without a serial cable and without flashing the separate diagnostic image. **A** runs the wiring test (~10 s): it walks the data and address lines of the PSRAM and of the 4 Mbit SRAM looking for open, stuck or shorted lines, and names the chip by its silkscreen — *U501: check data line D3*. **X** runs the full test (~30 s), which adds a write-and-verify pass over every cell and reports the first bad address if it finds one. The screen goes dark and the console resets while it runs — that is expected, the FPGA is loaded with the test core meanwhile — and the menu comes back with the result. Needs `fpga_test.bit`/`.bi3` on the card (it ships with the release); without it the test simply declines, without touching the running console. |
